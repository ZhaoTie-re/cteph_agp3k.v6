**Generated summary (findings).**

**Replicated associations.** Fibrinogen genes are the most consistently implicated: the FGA Thr312Ala variant was associated with CTEPH in three independent case–control studies [18057060, 23894515, 36856592], and an FGA 3'UTR Ins/Del allele (modulated by miR-759) in another [20677013]; FGG was identified at genome-wide level in two studies [38470220, 42877973]. Coagulation loci ABO [38470220, 39150410, 42877973], F11 [38470220, 39150410], F2 [38470220, 39150410] and F5 [38470220, 20182352] were each associated in two studies, together with MYH7B, TSPAN15, SLC44A2 and HLA-DRA [38470220]. Other single-study associations include ICAM1 E469K [31324443], SERPINE1 4G/5G [41596405], STAB2 rare variant enrichment [40726512], CLEC7A, TNFSF13B, LRP1, ETS1 and FGR by Mendelian randomisation [41915478], and CTSS by multiomic prioritisation [42877973]. GNB3 C825T [24531084, 26821322] and ACE genotype [16936432, 26821322] were associated with outcome/clinical worsening rather than susceptibility.

**Population-specific findings.** In Japanese cohorts, HLA-B*5201 and HLA-DPB1*0202 were more frequent in patients [15640334], the association being confined to deep-vein-thrombosis-negative CTEPH, together with NFKBIL1 IKBL-p*03 [19165231]; HLA-B*5201 status also stratified clinical phenotype, thrombus distribution and surgical outcome [18931448, 21930318]. ACE D-allele carriage predicted lower walk distance and survival in Japanese patients [16936432]. Chinese studies reported ACVRL1 c.1450C>G, enrichment of nonsynonymous variants in BMPR2/ACVRL1/ENG/SMAD9/CAV1/KCNK3/CBLN2 and the SNPs rs3739817 and rs55805125 [26820968], PROC/PROS1/SERPINC1 variants in thrombophilic patients [36338413], and novel GP6, TET2 and JAK2 variants in families [40221599].

**Acquired/clonal variants.** JAK2 V617F was repeatedly reported in CTEPH with myeloproliferative neoplasms [33024690, 35381612, 36949868, 42698555] and was enriched as a pathogenic variant in one cohort [32397294]. Clonal haematopoiesis mutations (DNMT3A, TET2, RUNX1, ASXL1) occurred in 9.4% of patients [38116660], and CH-associated variants in 20.1%, rising to 44.4% at age 80–89 [39604025].

**Negative candidates.** Factor V Leiden and prothrombin G20210A were not associated in several series [8883290, 10706510, 11555695, 25796019, 36338413, 41596405]; likewise PTGIS [15182267], BMPR2 [17287300, 41300788], NOS3 and HTR2A [19844076], OR2T3 [27122457], VKORC1/CYP2C9 [36856592], MTHFR and F13A1 V34L [41596405], ANO1 [42346503], and CPB2, FGA, VWF/F8 in one sequencing study [41300788].

**Strength of evidence.** Evidence is strongest for coagulation/fibrinogen loci (replicated GWAS) and Japanese HLA associations; most other genes rest on single studies, small samples or case reports, and thrombophilia findings are inconsistent [41300788, 11454828, 41063787, 41425438].

### Curated facts

- HLA (Japanese): HLA-B*52:01 (40% vs 24%) and DPB1*02:02 (19% vs 6%) are more frequent in Japanese CTEPH. (Tanabe 2005, PMID 15640334)
- HLA (Japanese): HLA markers (DPB1*02:02, B*52:01) and NFKBIL1 are associated with DVT-negative CTEPH only. (Kominami 2009, PMID 19165231)
- GWAS: The CTEPH GWAS: 1,907 European cases and 10,363 controls. (Liley 2024, PMID 38470220)
- GWAS: CTEPH is partially heritable and polygenic, with genetics related to but distinct from PE and DVT. (Liley 2024, PMID 38470220)
- Epidemiology (Japan): In the worldwide registry, 75% of Japanese CTEPH patients were female. (Guth 2021, PMID 34409094)
- Epidemiology (Japan): Japanese women with CTEPH have less DVT and fewer acute embolic episodes. (Shigeta 2008, PMID 18931448)

**Generated summary (methods).**

**Designs over time.** The earliest CTEPH genetics was dominated by candidate-gene association studies of haemostatic variants and by single-patient reports [8883290, 10706510, 11454828, 11555695, 11796466]. From the mid-2000s HLA typing and microsatellite mapping of the HLA region appeared [15640334, 18931448, 19165231, 21930318], followed by pharmacogenetic genotype-stratified treatment analyses [24531084, 26821322]. After 2015 the field moved to targeted next-generation sequencing panels of pulmonary arterial hypertension and thrombophilia genes [26820968, 32397294, 36338413, 41300788], whole-exome and whole-genome sequencing with family/linkage designs [27122457, 30047301, 40221599, 40726512], clonal-haematopoiesis/somatic analyses [38116660, 39604025], and finally GWAS and Mendelian randomisation [38470220, 41163636, 41915478, 42877973].

**Populations.** Japanese cohorts were prominent early, covering PTGIS VNTR, ACE I/D and HLA studies [15182267, 16936432, 15640334, 18931448, 19165231, 21930318] and later exome screening [33103541]. Other East Asian work used Han Chinese cohorts for FGA variants, PAH-gene panels, pedigree WGS and CTEPH GWAS [23894515, 26820968, 40221599, 42877973]. European, German, Swiss, Turkish, Czech and US cohorts followed [32397294, 19844076, 36856592, 39150410, 40726512]; population ancestry was frequently unstated.

**Sample sizes and controls.** Cases ranged from single patients to nearly 2000 [34317315, 41163636], with a median near 51. Controls were heterogeneous: healthy volunteers [18057060, 36856592, 42346503], other pulmonary hypertension or post-embolism patients without PH [10706510, 17287300, 20182352, 26820968, 41300788], surgical lung specimens [31324443], or public databases such as gnomAD, ToMMo, 3.5KJPN and UK Biobank [32397294, 33103541, 39604025, 40726512].

**Platforms and quality control.** Methods spanned PCR/RFLP and TaqMan or FRET real-time PCR [10706510, 23894515, 36856592, 39150410, 41596405, 42346503], MassARRAY genotyping [30047301], Sanger sequencing [36338413], and Illumina exome/panel/genome sequencing with BWA, GATK, MuTect2, ANNOVAR and ACMG/HGVS classification, CADD and in-silico prediction filters, depth and variant-allele-frequency thresholds, and Hardy–Weinberg checks [33103541, 39604025, 40221599, 40726512, 41300788]. GWAS used four arrays, ancestry principal components and Haplotype Reference Consortium imputation [38470220].

**Statistics and limits.** Most studies compared genotype and allele frequencies by chi-square/Fisher tests with odds ratios and logistic regression [18057060, 23894515, 36338413, 39150410], occasionally adjusting for haemodynamics, age or sex [16936432, 26821322, 39604025]. Formal multiple-testing correction (Pc, Holm, Benjamini–Hochberg, genome-wide thresholds) was used only in some studies [19165231, 33103541, 38470220, 41163636], and explicit replication was rare [27122457, 38470220, 40221599, 40726512]. Limits recurring in the material include unreported ancestry, small or absent control groups, non-healthy comparison groups, abstract-only method reporting, and lack of hemodynamic confirmation of CTEPH [41596405].

## 10. Caveats

- Relations to CTEPH are evidence of association (clinical studies, ontology, shared genetic signal), not of causation. Many classic risk factors were found against non-thromboembolic PH controls; against PE survivors most were not enriched (Dodson 2022, PMID 35000431).
- Colocalisation here is eCAVIAR on PICS fine-mapping (CLPP); a trait sharing only the ABO locus mostly reflects the pleiotropy of ABO, so single-locus relations are weaker than multi-locus ones.
- Locus-to-gene (L2G) scores are predictions of the causal gene, not proof.
- Curated gene evidence comes from curated resources (ClinGen, PanelApp, Orphanet, ClinVar, UniProt, Gene2Phenotype, gene burden) for the condition or its descendants in the ontology; it is mostly rare-variant, Mendelian evidence.
- The literature studies were screened and extracted by a language model from PubMed abstracts and open-access Methods sections; every gene, variant, number and quote was checked against the source text, and items that failed were dropped. Studies behind paywalls contribute their abstract only.
- Gene themes of the CTEPH literature are an editorial grouping; "associated in two or more studies" counts studies, not independent samples.
- Generated summaries are syntheses of the listed source records; they cite only those records, but they are not a systematic review.
- Text-mined target links can map an abbreviation to the wrong gene; the matched words are listed in the workbook.
